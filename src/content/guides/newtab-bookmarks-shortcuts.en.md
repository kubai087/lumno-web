Keep the sites you use most under the search box, one click away, and browse your bookmarks without digging through folders.

## Shortcuts {#shortcuts}

The row of icons under the search box shows your shortcuts. When you hover over them, they magnify like the macOS Dock.

![A row of shortcut icons under the search box; the Figma icon under the pointer is magnified with its name shown](nt-shortcuts "Your favorite sites, one click away")

### Add a shortcut

1. Click "+" at the end of your shortcuts and choose Add site.
2. Enter a name and URL. Browser pages such as `chrome://inspect/#devices` are also supported.
3. Choose an icon: a built-in icon, an online icon, or your own image (a square image of at least 128×128 px works best).
4. Click Save.

You can also add the page you're on: right-click an empty part of the page and choose Add to Lumno New Tab shortcuts, or click the Lumno icon in the toolbar and choose Add shortcut in the [popup](../install/#toolbar-popup). The page is added to the end of your last shortcut group.

### Icon backgrounds

Some sites have transparent or non-square icons. Lumno puts these on a plate tinted with the icon's main color, so they look like the other square icons.

In a shortcut's edit dialog, you can turn off "Add a background to irregular icons", or change Background color from From the icon to Custom color. The palette button lets you pick any color, enter a HEX / RGB value, or use a color you saved.

### Organize shortcuts

- Right-click a shortcut to edit or remove it. If you remove one by mistake, use the shortcut shown in the notice to undo.
- Drag shortcuts to reorder them. Drag one onto your bookmarks to turn it into a bookmark. You can also select a shortcut with `Tab` and press `⌥ ←` or `⌥ →` to move it one place.
- To change how many shortcuts appear per row, icon size (up to 112), spacing, Dock magnification or the "+" button, use the Shortcuts section of the new tab's Appearance settings, or Appearance → New Tab in Lumno's settings. You can also hide "+" by clicking it and choosing Hide.
- You can add up to 200 shortcuts.
- If you don't need shortcuts, turn off Shortcuts in the same place to remove the row of icons under the search box.

## Shortcut groups {#shortcut-groups}

When you have a lot of shortcuts, sort them into groups such as Work, AI and Everyday, each with its own title.

![Shortcuts under the search box in three groups, Work, AI and Everyday; the pointer rests on the Work title, which shows its "···" button](nt-shortcut-groups "Shortcuts sorted into groups")

1. Click "+" at the end of your shortcuts and choose New group.
2. Enter a name for the group and click Save. The name is optional; a group without one just starts a new row.

- Hover over a group's title and click "···" on its right to rename the group, add a site to it, or move it up or down. You can also double-click a title to rename it.
- Drag a group's title to reorder your groups. Drag a shortcut into another group to move it there.
- Delete group moves its sites into the group above. Delete group and sites asks first, then removes both. Press `⌘ Z` to undo either one.
- You can create up to 20 groups.

## Shortcut folders {#shortcut-folders}

When your shortcuts pile up, stack them into folders, like apps on a phone's home screen. Drag one shortcut onto the middle of another, wait for "Release to create a folder in the bookmarks bar", then let go. Dropping it on either side of an icon just reorders.

![The third shortcut under the search box is a folder; opened, it lists Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini and Perplexity from the "AI" folder](nt-shortcut-folder "An open shortcut folder")

- The new folder is saved at the end of your browser's bookmarks bar and takes the place of the shortcut you dropped onto. Press `⌘ Z` to undo.
- Drag a shortcut onto a folder to put it inside. You can also drag a folder from the bookmarks area into your shortcuts.
- Click a folder to show its sites, and click it again to close it.
- Right-click a site in a folder and choose Edit to give it an icon, just like a shortcut. The icon is saved on this device only.
- Right-click a folder to open all of its pages in a new tab group, rename it, change its color, or remove it from your shortcuts. The folder stays in your bookmarks bar.

## Bookmarks {#bookmarks}

The bookmarks area shows the contents of your browser's bookmarks bar. When you hover over a folder, its card shows the icons of a few sites inside it.

![The new tab's bookmarks area: folders such as Reference, Design guidelines and Competitors, plus ChatGPT and GitHub bookmarks. The Reference folder under the pointer shows the icons of the sites inside it](nt-bookmarks "The bookmarks area")

- Right-click a folder to change its color. The folder also uses that color in menus.
- Right-click a folder and choose "Open in new tab group" to open all of its pages at once, grouped together.
- Right-click a bookmark or folder and choose "Add to shortcuts" to add it to the end of your last shortcut group. The bookmark stays where it is. If it's already among your shortcuts, the menu says "Already in shortcuts".
- Click the bookmark icon to open the browser's bookmark manager.
- Shortcuts and bookmarks open in the current tab by default. To open them in a new tab instead, turn each one on under General → New Tab Settings → Open in a new tab in Lumno's settings. Holding `⌘` while you click, or middle-clicking, always opens in the background.

## Bookmark layouts {#bookmark-layouts}

Click "···" at the top right of the bookmarks area to switch between three layouts.

### Multi-layer folder view

The default layout. Folders and bookmarks appear as cards. Click a folder to open it in place; the heading shows where you are, such as "Bookmarks / Reference". Click "Bookmarks" to go back up a level.

![Multi-layer folder view with the Reference folder open: the heading reads "Bookmarks / Reference", above a CSS layout subfolder and two bookmarks](nt-bookmarks-folder "Multi-layer folder view: a folder opens in place")

### Multi-level list view

The cards stay where they are. Click a folder to open it as a menu; subfolders open to the right as you go. You can browse deeply nested folders without leaving the page you're on.

![Multi-level list view: clicking the Reference folder opens a menu, and the CSS layout subfolder under the pointer opens to the right with web.dev and Can I use](nt-bookmarks-list "Multi-level list view: menus open level by level")

### Top bookmarks bar

Bookmarks appear in a row along the top of the page, like the browser's own bookmarks bar. Folders in the bar open as menus too.

![The top bookmarks bar layout, with bookmark folders in a row along the top of the new tab](nt-bookmarks-top "Top bookmarks bar")

With the top bookmarks bar, click "···" at the right end of the bar to choose its style and background:

- Bar style: Docked to top sits flush against the top of the browser; Floating is a rounded bar set slightly below it.
- Bar background: Match style, Transparent or Custom color. Match style gives the bar the same [material](../newtab-wallpaper/#glass) as the rest of the new tab.

## Rows and hiding bookmarks {#bookmark-rows}

To choose how many rows of bookmarks appear and how many fit in each row, use the Bookmarks section of the new tab's Appearance settings, or Appearance → New Tab in Lumno's settings.

![Four settings in Lumno: Bookmark rows, Bookmarks per row, Show Icons Inside Bookmark Folders and Site Cards](nt-layout-settings "Bookmark settings under Appearance → New Tab")

- Bookmark rows: choose from 0 to 8 rows. The default is 2. When your bookmarks don't all fit, use the arrows at the top right of the bookmarks area to page through them.
- Hide bookmarks: drag Bookmark rows to 0. The new tab no longer shows your bookmarks, and the top bookmarks bar is hidden too. Your browser's bookmarks aren't affected, and you can bring them back at any time.
- Bookmarks per row: choose from 4 to 8. The default is 6. This is the most that appear when the window is wide enough; narrower windows show fewer.
- Show Icons Inside Bookmark Folders: turn this off to stop showing site icons when you hover over a folder.

The site cards at the bottom of the new tab are set here too. See [Frequent sites and progress cards](../newtab-frequent-sites/#card-rows).
