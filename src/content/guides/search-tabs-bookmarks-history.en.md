With dozens of tabs open and hundreds of bookmarks saved, finding a page can take longer than opening it. Lumno puts them all in one box: press a shortcut, type a few letters, and everything open, saved and visited shows up together.

## Open the search box {#open}

Press `⌘ ⇧ K` (`Ctrl + Shift + K` on Windows and Linux) on any page. The box appears in the middle of the screen. Press `Esc` or click outside to close it.

On restricted pages like `chrome://` settings or extension stores, browsers don't let extensions show anything. Lumno then follows the "Restricted page behavior" option in its settings.

## Everything in one list {#results}

The label beside each result tells you where it came from:

- Opened: a tab you have open. Press Return to switch to it instead of opening a duplicate.
- Bookmark: a page from your bookmarks bar or folders, with the folder shown.
- Frequent and History: the sites you visit most, and your browsing history.

Your search engine's suggestions appear too; press Return to search. The results you pick most rise to the top.

![Lumno opened on a blank page: typing chrome lists an open GitHub tab, search suggestions, a bookmark and top sites](search "Type chrome: what's open, saved and visited, together")

A couple of handy keys: `⌥ ↩` opens in the current tab, and `⇧ ↩` opens another copy of an open tab. All keys are in [Keyboard shortcuts](../keyboard-shortcuts/#command-bar-keys).

## Search only your open tabs {#open-tabs}

When there are just too many tabs, narrow it down: press `Tab` once while the box is empty. "Search open tabs" appears on the left and only your open tabs remain. Type to filter, press Return to switch, and `Esc` to leave.

![The search box shows "Search open tabs" on the left, with only the currently open tabs listed](opentabs "Search open tabs only")

To search only bookmarks, history or top sites, press `Tab` twice in an empty box and pick one in the search scope panel.
